# MOO Engine: Recovering the Implicit Values Behind Singapore's COE Policy

## 0. Read this first

Personal project, three week budget, solo. Deliverable is a deployed link, a public repo,
and a written case study. Audience is product hiring managers, not academics.

The optimiser is not the point. Non-dominated sorting is solved and pymoo does it in one
call. What is demonstrated is the ability to take a contested public argument, force it
into an explicit formulation, and be honest about what that formulation cost. Every
shortcut is visible in the output, not buried in a notebook.

**Headline claim:** Singapore's COE settings imply a specific ratio of how much the state
values congestion relief against car affordability against revenue. That ratio was never
published. It can be recovered from public data.

**Why it is timely.** The zero vehicle growth rate for Categories A, B and D is legislated
only until 31 January 2028. This is not a counterfactual exercise about a settled policy.
It is about a parameter that comes up for decision inside two years.

## 1. The argument

People reject viable policies because they optimise for different values, then mistake a
difference in preference for an objective failure. There is a real category of failed
policy, and a much larger category that is simply weighted differently, and public argument
does not distinguish between them.

Pareto efficiency draws the line. A policy worse on every objective than some other
available policy is a genuine failure. A policy on the frontier is one where you cannot
improve any objective without giving up another, and whether it is the right answer depends
on what you weight.

COE is the test case because the Singapore argument about it is exactly this confusion. A
tradesman who needs a van, a family that wants a car, and a commuter who wants clear roads
all read the others' preferred settings as proof the system is broken. They are describing
three different points on the same frontier.

## 3. The model

### 3.1 Decision variables

**This section was rebuilt after the day one scan. Read the reasoning, not just the
variable list.**

The original specification used quota counts as decision variables. That was wrong. Under
the current system the quota is not chosen. For each category and quarter it is:

```
Quota = growth allowance
      + (rolling 4-quarter deregistrations, net of guaranteed deregistrations) / 4
      + adjustments (taxi population change, expired TCOEs, Early Turnover Scheme,
                     guaranteed-deregistration redistribution, discretionary injection)
```

**The 25 percent in the replacement term is not a policy rate.** It is one quarter of the
trailing annual figure, converting an annual count into a quarterly quota. Do not treat it
as a lever. It is arithmetic.

So the decision variables are the **policy parameters that feed the formula**, not its
output.

**Recommended set, three variables:**

- `g_ab` — annual vehicle growth rate applied to Categories A and B. Currently 0 percent,
  fixed since February 2018, legislated to 31 January 2028.
- `g_c` — annual vehicle growth rate applied to Category C. Currently 0.25 percent.
- `theta` — the Category A share of car demand, moved by shifting the power output
  threshold separating A from B. Currently 97kW for non-electric and 110kW for fully
  electric cars.

Quota per category then falls out of the formula, premiums fall out of the fits in section
4, and the objectives follow.

**Why this set and not something simpler.** There is a collinearity trap here. Growth rate,
replacement adjustments and discretionary injections all push total quota the same
direction. Using several of them as separate variables would give three levers pointing one
way, all three objectives would become functions of total quota, and the frontier would
collapse into a traced line. Exactly the failure that killed the housing scenario.

The set above avoids it because the three levers move in genuinely different directions.
`g_ab` changes total car volume. `g_c` reallocates between private and commercial vehicles,
which differ in premium level, revenue per COE and road load per vehicle. `theta`
reallocates within cars without changing total car population at all.

**Check this before building on it.** Sample the space, compute the three objectives, and
confirm the resulting front is a surface rather than a curve. This is assumption A-08 and it
is the first thing to verify after the fits are done. If `theta` turns out unmodellable
because demand by power output is not published, fall back to a discretionary injection
lever and accept that the frontier will be flatter.

**Bounds.** Anchor on what has actually happened rather than what is arithmetically
possible.

- `g_ab`: the growth rate has stepped down through several regimes across the scheme's life,
  ending at 0 percent from February 2018. Extract the actual historical values and use that
  envelope, extended modestly at both ends.
- `g_c`: currently 0.25 percent, same approach.
- `theta`: bound by the observed distribution of car registrations by power output.

If a discretionary injection lever is used instead, the calibration anchors are the roughly
20,000 COEs announced from February 2025 spread over several years, and the 30,813 COEs
redistributed and injected in total between May 2023 and November 2025.

Record all bounds in the assumptions register with the historical basis for each.

### 3.2 Objectives

Minimisation form internally, signs flipped at presentation.

**O1. Ownership cost.** Volume-weighted mean quota premium across categories. The buyer's
objective.

**O2. Congestion.** Quota propagates to vehicle population, population to road load, load to
average peak-hour speed. See 4.3.

**O3. Revenue.** Total premium collected. See 4.4 for the reconciliation test that now
exists for this.

**On whether revenue belongs here.** The scan settled the factual question. No Singapore
minister has framed COE as a revenue instrument. The stated purpose is consistent from the
1990 Parliamentary Select Committee on Land Transport, through the 1996 White Paper "A World
Class Land Transport System", to ministerial statements in 2023 and 2025: COE is an
allocation mechanism for a fixed vehicle quota under land scarcity. The closest any minister
comes to the revenue dimension is describing what the revenue is spent on, which is not the
same as describing what it is for.

Revenue stays in the model anyway, for the same two reasons as before. The premise of the
tool is that stated intent and implied values can differ, so restricting the objective set to
officially declared aims builds something that can only reproduce the official account. And
revenue is a real consequence regardless of intent, at several billion dollars a year, which
constrains what policy changes are survivable.

Revenue is a **finding, not an assumption**. See 5.3.

### 3.3 Constraints

Constraints are where value judgements openly live and they stay separate from dominance in
the code and the UI. A policy excluded by a constraint is not a failed policy, it is one you
ruled out.

At minimum: a floor on Category C so commercial vehicles are not optimised to zero, and the
bounds from 3.1. Every constraint carries a one line justification in config, and the
frontend renders constraint-excluded and dominated regions distinctly.

## 4. What has to be fitted, and how honest to be about it

Nothing here is invented. Every functional form is fitted to published data or cited to
literature. Where a form is chosen for tractability rather than accuracy, that is stated in
the output.

### 4.1 Quota to premium, and the elasticity problem

Fit from LTA bidding results, two exercises per month per category from April 2002.

**The auction is uniform price.** Bidders submit confidential reserve prices, the current
price increments until bids at or above it equal the quota, and every successful bidder in a
category pays the same premium, set at the highest unsuccessful bid plus one dollar. This
supports treating the observed premium as a market-clearing quantity rather than an average
of heterogeneous payments, which is what makes the reduced form defensible at all.

Start with a log-log specification per category:

```
ln(P_i) = a_i + b_i * ln(Q_i) + controls
```

**Do not fit a single elasticity across the full sample.** There is a widely made argument
that COE stopped functioning as a deterrent as buyers absorbed the premium as an assumed cost
of ownership. If that is right, price elasticity fell over time and one regression across
2002 to 2026 averages two behavioural regimes and describes neither.

- Fit `b_i` on rolling windows or with a time-varying coefficient, and plot the path.
- If the elasticity flattens, you have measured the phenomenon rather than repeating it. This
  is a result worth reporting on its own.
- Build the frontier on the current regime only.
- Expect the shape to differ from the naive version. Inelastic demand means added quota
  barely moves price, so revenue trends closer to linear in quota rather than peaking, and the
  cost against congestion trade-off steepens.

**Category coupling.** Categories are not independent. Category E supply is set at 10 percent
of the summed Category A, B and C replacement quotas, and Category E is almost always used for
larger cars, so it acts as an arbitrage channel transmitting demand pressure back into A and
B. The 10 percent supply linkage is published. No official spillover coefficient exists, so
any demand-side spillover in the model is your estimate and must be labelled as such. Given
the timeline, model Category E supply mechanically and treat demand spillover as an
acknowledged limitation rather than trying to estimate it.

**The caveat that ships with the fit.** COE premiums are a bid outcome, not a function of
quota. They move on demand shifts, deregistration cycles, category migration, credit
conditions and policy announcements. Quota is now known to be formula-driven off
deregistrations, which weakens the simple endogeneity worry but introduces a mechanical
relationship between the supply series and the vehicle stock that must be handled carefully.
This is a reduced form, not a structural demand estimate. State it in the case study and in a
tooltip on the chart.

**Structural breaks to handle explicitly:**

| Date | Break |
|---|---|
| April 2002 | Closed to open bidding. Left-truncate the series here. |
| August 2012 | Taxis moved out of the bidding system, drawing from Category E at Category A prevailing price. |
| February 2014 | Category A gains a 97kW power output criterion alongside the 1,600cc limit. |
| May 2017 | Category D deregistrations stop feeding Category E. |
| April to June 2020 | Bidding exercises suspended. 19,490 COEs redistributed over 24 exercises from July 2020 to July 2021. |
| May 2022 | Category A threshold for fully electric cars raised to 110kW. |
| February 2023 | Quota computation moves to a rolling four-quarter deregistration average. Supply-side regime change, not a data-field change. |
| May 2023 | Cut-and-fill redistribution begins, bringing forward guaranteed deregistrations. |
| February 2025 | Discretionary injection begins. |

Published datasets flag some of these in footnotes and not others. There is no
machine-readable break column. Build the break table yourself from the above, verify each
date against a primary source, and treat the table as part of the deliverable.

### 4.2 Quota to vehicle population

A COE runs ten years, so the stock is roughly a rolling window of the last decade of
registrations. Model it as an accumulator: prior population, minus deregistrations, plus
released quota.

Complications the scan surfaced. COEs can be renewed by paying the prevailing quota premium,
which breaks the clean ten year window. Five year non-extendable COEs exist and were the
first source drawn on by cut-and-fill. Early deregistration is common and is what the
"guaranteed deregistration" term in the quota formula refers to.

Backtest it. Run it forward over the historical record and compare against the published
population series. If it does not reproduce history, it is wrong and everything above it is
worthless.

### 4.3 Population to congestion

Use the standard BPR volume-delay function rather than inventing a relationship:

```
speed = free_flow_speed / (1 + alpha * (V/C)^beta)
```

with conventional alpha and beta, cited.

**What the data supports, and the problem with it.** LTA publishes average peak hour speeds
split into expressways and arterial roads, annually, from 2004, with peak hour defined as 8
to 9am and 6 to 7pm on weekdays. Expressway speeds have run in the low sixties, arterial
roads in the mid to high twenties. LTA has cited target bands of 45 to 65 km/h for
expressways and 20 to 30 km/h for arterial roads.

That gives roughly twenty annual observations, across a period when the vehicle population
moved slowly and mostly in one direction. Calibrating a volume-delay curve from that is weak
and the fitted `beta` will be poorly identified. **Expect O2 to be the least defensible of
the three objectives and say so rather than hiding it.** This is assumption A-09.

Capacity anchors on published lane-kilometres by road category. Note a large single-year jump
in expressway and total lane-km between 2023 and 2024 that looks like reclassification rather
than construction. Flag it, do not smooth over it. Note also that published road length covers
only roads maintained by LTA and excludes those maintained by other agencies.

Any parameter that cannot be calibrated goes in config, marked as an assumption, and into the
sensitivity sweep.

### 4.4 Revenue reconciliation

The Ministry of Finance reports "Vehicle Quota Premiums" as a distinct line in its annual
Analysis of Revenue and Expenditure, separate from Motor Vehicle Taxes. This gives the model a
ground-truth series it did not have before.

Reconcile your computed revenue against it. Compute quota times premium summed across
categories for a historical year and compare against the published figure. Agreement within a
reasonable margin validates both the premium series handling and the quota accounting.

Two things to get right. The MOF line covers **all** categories including D and E, so the
reconciliation must sum all five even though only A, B and C are decision dimensions. And the
figures are fiscal years, so align the periods before comparing.

If the reconciliation fails badly, that is a bug in your pipeline, not a finding about MOF.

## 5. The engine

### 5.1 Front generation

pymoo, NSGA-II, three objectives, three decision variables. Small problem, seconds to run.

Sample the feasible space densely and keep the full cloud. The cloud is the product, not
debug output. Showing every possible policy at once with the frontier as the boundary of what
is achievable is a better rendering of the argument than a filtered table. The dominated
interior is what a zero looks like.

### 5.2 The inverse weight query

For each front point, determine the weight vectors under which it is optimal.

Sample the weight simplex on a grid, compute the weighted sum across normalised objectives
for each weight vector, record which front point wins, store the mapping. Two precomputed
arrays and a nested loop. No solver, runs offline.

With three objectives the weight vector is a triangle, so the whole preference space renders
as a ternary plot coloured by winning policy. Plotly supports ternary charts.

Two things to handle rather than hide:

- **Normalisation matters here.** Dominance is invariant to monotone rescaling of a single
  objective. Weighted sums are not. The ternary map depends on how the three objectives were
  put on a common scale. Pick a method and state it prominently. This is where the tool's own
  value judgement enters and it should be labelled.
- **Concavity.** Points in a concave region win under no weight vector, because a weighted sum
  cannot reach into a concavity. Those policies are non-dominated yet never optimal under any
  linear preference. Detect them, mark them differently, write about them.

### 5.3 The headline output

Locate the current policy settings in the decision space. Under the rebuilt variables that
means `g_ab` at 0 percent, `g_c` at 0.25 percent, and `theta` at the current 97kW and 110kW
thresholds. Find the nearest front point and read off the implied weight region.

**Report the revenue weight as a finding.** Near zero supports the official account that COE
is a population control instrument. Substantial supports the revenue critique. Do not decide
in advance which you expect.

Then do it by era. The growth rate has stepped down through several distinct regimes over the
scheme's life, so each is a separately observed policy position. Recover the implied weights
for each and watch whether the revenue weight drifts. Drift would be your own evidence for a
claim that currently rests on commentary.

If the actual policy sits well off the frontier, that is a finding about the model's
omissions, not about LTA, and it is reported that way.

### 5.4 Validation

Three tests, all passing before anything is trusted.

- **Optimiser correctness.** ZDT1 for two objectives, DTLZ2 for three. Compare generated
  points against the analytically known front and report the error. Lives in the test suite,
  needs no UI.
- **Accumulator backtest.** Reproduce the published vehicle population series.
- **Revenue reconciliation.** Against the MOF Vehicle Quota Premiums line. See 4.4.

The answer to "how do you know this is right" should not be "the picture looked plausible."

## 6. Sensitivity

Required, not extra. Everything rests on fitted coefficients carrying real uncertainty, and
after the scan we know O2's coefficients are the weakest.

Perturb coefficients across their confidence intervals, regenerate the frontier repeatedly,
render as a band rather than a line. Narrow band, robust conclusion. Wide band, say so.

Do the same for the headline claim. If the recovered ratio moves from 3:1 to 1.5:1 across
plausible parameter values, the honest statement is a range.

Run the congestion parameters through this with particular care, since they are the least
well identified.

## 7. Architecture

Static. There is no server.

```
Python build script  ->  JSON artifacts  ->  static frontend
```

Frontier, feasible cloud, weight simplex map and sensitivity bands are computed offline and
written to JSON. The site loads and renders them. Weight sliders need no backend because
weights only select a point on a front that already exists.

The accepted trade-off is that users cannot move constraints and watch the feasible region
reshape. That needs live re-optimisation. It is a real loss and it is worth it for having no
infrastructure to run, pay for or explain.

### Repo layout

```
/data/raw            downloaded LTA, SingStat and MOF files, committed for reproducibility
/data/processed      cleaned series
/src/fit             the fitted models from section 4
/src/model           quota formula, objectives, constraints, problem definition
/src/optimise        pymoo runs, front generation, weight simplex map
/src/export          JSON artifact writers
/tests               ZDT1, DTLZ2, accumulator backtest, revenue reconciliation
/web                 static frontend
/docs                brief, assumptions register, decision log, break table, case study
```

### Stack

Python with pymoo, numpy, pandas, statsmodels or scikit-learn. Plotly for charting, chosen
over D3 because the timeline is three weeks. Static hosting from the repo.

No dependencies beyond this without a reason in the decision log.

## 11. Framing, and what not to claim

Rittel and Webber define wicked problems partly by having no definitive formulation and no
stopping rule. Multi-objective optimisation requires a fixed formulation, fixed objectives and
stable evaluation functions. This project does not solve a wicked problem. It forces one into a
tame formulation and makes the choices involved visible and contestable.

That is the more interesting claim and the one to make. "We model wicked problems" is the first
thing anyone who knows the literature will attack.

**The deepest limitation, and it belongs in the writeup rather than the code.** The policy
changed the behaviour it was measuring. If COE stopped deterring because buyers absorbed it as
an assumed cost, then the mapping from quota to outcomes drifted because of the intervention
itself. MOO assumes that mapping is fixed. It is not, which means the frontier moves as you
move along it. Fitting on the recent regime assumes the current elasticity holds while you push
the growth rate to the edges of its range, which is exactly the assumption the last twenty
years falsified. There is no fix inside the method. Write it up properly.

**Four things that must not be claimed:**

- That this predicts COE prices
- That the recovered weights are LTA's internal reasoning, rather than what the policy implies
  under this specific model
- That the government reframed COE as a revenue instrument. The scan searched for this
  specifically and found no such statement. The documented purpose, consistent from 1990 to
  2025, is vehicle population allocation under land scarcity. Ministers have described what the
  revenue funds, which is not the same as describing it as a purpose. Present the revenue
  critique as commentary, or let the recovered weight speak to it.
- That COE revenue is hypothecated to any particular use. Ministers have described the revenue
  as going toward public transport subsidy and general expenditure. That is a statement about
  spending, not an earmark, and no primary document reviewed states the destination in
  fund-accounting terms.

## 12. The case study

Structure it around the reasoning, not the output. Most portfolio projects show what was built
and none show why.

- The argument, in a paragraph
- Why COE, and what was rejected. A public health version was dropped because it would have
  reproduced a published frontier. A housing version was dropped because affordability and
  density mostly do not conflict, so there was no frontier to plot.
- **The day one scan and what it broke.** The original specification treated quota as a policy
  lever, which the published formula falsified within hours. Show the rebuild. This is the most
  useful thing in the document for a hiring manager, because it is evidence of how you handle
  being wrong.
- The scope cuts with reasons. Three objectives rather than more, because dominance gets sparse
  in higher dimensions. Static rather than live. One scenario rather than two.
- What was fitted, what was assumed, what sensitivity did to the conclusions. Be specific that
  the congestion objective is the weakest, and why.
- The elasticity path, if it shows anything
- The headline result, with its range
- **Limitations and future work.** Everything found after the week one freeze lands here. This
  section is a deliverable, not an apology.
